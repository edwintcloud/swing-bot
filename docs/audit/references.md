# References

Accessed 2026-08-25 unless otherwise noted. Links point to publishers, government records, data owners, or DOI records. A citation supports the stated proposition, not a guarantee of future performance.

## High-Return Claims and Performance Presentation

1. US Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations. *Abuse of Structured Financial Products: Misusing Basket Options to Avoid Taxes and Leverage Limits*. Hearing, July 22, 2014. [GovInfo hearing record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-113shrg89882/html/CHRG-113shrg89882.htm). Primary government record for Renaissance basket-option activity, trading scale, profits, and leverage. It is not a reproducible strategy specification or a complete audited return series.
2. Securities and Exchange Commission. *Investment Adviser Marketing*, Release IA-5653, effective May 4, 2021. [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2021/03/05/2020-28868/investment-adviser-marketing). Defines hypothetical performance to include model, backtested, targeted, and projected results and discusses hindsight and audience safeguards.
3. Commodity Futures Trading Commission. *17 CFR 4.41: Advertising by commodity pool operators, commodity trading advisors, and the principals thereof*. [eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-I/part-4/section-4.41). Official hypothetical-performance disclosure standard, including hindsight and liquidity limitations.
4. National Futures Association. *Compliance Rule 2-29: Communications with the Public and Promotional Material*. [NFA Rulebook](https://www.nfa.futures.org/rulebooksql/rules.aspx?Section=4&RuleID=RULE%202-29). Presentation and substantiation requirements for performance claims.

## Momentum and Trend

5. Moskowitz, Tobias J.; Ooi, Yao Hua; Pedersen, Lasse H. "Time Series Momentum." *Journal of Financial Economics* 104(2), 2012, 228-250. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2011.11.003). Documents own-return trend predictability across 58 futures and forwards over more than 25 years.
6. Hurst, Brian; Ooi, Yao Hua; Pedersen, Lasse H. "A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following Investing." *Journal of Portfolio Management* 44(1), 2017, 15-29. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3905/jpm.2017.44.1.015). Historical reconstruction of diversified trend-following evidence; reconstructed returns are not a century of live fund performance.
7. Jegadeesh, Narasimhan; Titman, Sheridan. "Returns to Buying Winners and Selling Losers: Implications for Stock Market Efficiency." *Journal of Finance* 48(1), 1993, 65-91. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1993.tb04702.x). Canonical cross-sectional equity momentum evidence.
8. Jegadeesh, Narasimhan; Titman, Sheridan. "Profitability of Momentum Strategies: An Evaluation of Alternative Explanations." *Journal of Finance* 56(2), 2001, 699-720. [NBER working-paper page](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7159). Examines persistence beyond the original sample and later reversal.
9. Asness, Clifford S.; Moskowitz, Tobias J.; Pedersen, Lasse H. "Value and Momentum Everywhere." *Journal of Finance* 68(3), 2013, 929-985. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12021). Documents value and momentum premia and their negative correlation across markets and asset classes.
10. Kenneth R. French Data Library. *Momentum Factor (Mom)*. [Definition](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/Data_Library/det_mom_factor.html) and [data library](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html). Public research-factor construction and downloadable histories; not investor fund returns.

## Reversal, Value, and Quality

11. Jegadeesh, Narasimhan. "Evidence of Predictable Behavior of Security Returns." *Journal of Finance* 45(3), 1990, 881-898. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1990.tb05098.x). Short-horizon serial dependence and reversal-related evidence.
12. De Bondt, Werner F. M.; Thaler, Richard. "Does the Stock Market Overreact?" *Journal of Finance* 40(3), 1985, 793-805. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1985.tb05004.x). Long-horizon winner/loser reversal evidence.
13. Fama, Eugene F.; French, Kenneth R. "The Cross-Section of Expected Stock Returns." *Journal of Finance* 47(2), 1992, 427-465. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1992.tb04398.x). Canonical size and book-to-market evidence.
14. Fama, Eugene F.; French, Kenneth R. "A Five-Factor Asset Pricing Model." *Journal of Financial Economics* 116(1), 2015, 1-22. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2014.10.010). Adds profitability and investment factors.
15. Kenneth R. French Data Library. *Description of Fama/French 5 Factors (2x3)*. [Definition](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/Data_Library/f-f_5_factors_2x3.html). Official factor construction, eligible universe, and data periods.
16. Novy-Marx, Robert. "The Other Side of Value: The Gross Profitability Premium." *Journal of Financial Economics* 108(1), 2013, 1-28. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2013.01.003). Gross-profitability evidence relevant to quality selection.

## Portfolio Construction and Baselines

17. Asness, Clifford S.; Frazzini, Andrea; Pedersen, Lasse H. "Leverage Aversion and Risk Parity." *Financial Analysts Journal* 68(1), 2012, 47-59. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2469/faj.v68.n1.1). Economic rationale and evidence for risk-balanced allocation; risk parity is a portfolio framework, not an alpha guarantee.
18. Bessembinder, Hendrik. "Do Stocks Outperform Treasury Bills?" *Review of Financial Studies* 31(3), 2018, 1085-1115. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhy020). Shows that aggregate US stock wealth creation is concentrated in a small minority of stocks.

## Multiple Testing and Backtest Risk

19. Harvey, Campbell R.; Liu, Yan; Zhu, Heqing. "... and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns." *Review of Financial Studies* 29(1), 2016, 5-68. [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhv059). Shows why conventional significance thresholds are inadequate after extensive factor mining and proposes a higher hurdle.
20. Bailey, David H.; López de Prado, Marcos. "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio: Correcting for Selection Bias, Backtest Overfitting, and Non-Normality." *Journal of Portfolio Management* 40(5), 2014, 94-107. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3905/jpm.2014.40.5.094). Adjusts Sharpe evidence for multiple trials and non-normal returns.
21. Bailey, David H.; Borwein, Jonathan M.; López de Prado, Marcos; Zhu, Qiji Jim. "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting." *Journal of Computational Finance* 20(4), 2017. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21314/JCF.2017.322). Formalizes how model selection can choose an in-sample winner that fails out of sample.
